# Shared: CEFR Descriptors — Academic/Scientific Language Production
# Used by: all agents (level calibration)
# Source: CEFR 2001/2020, CEFR-AW (Academic Writing) extension

## B1 — Threshold
**Written:** Can write simple, connected texts on familiar topics.
Limited range of academic vocabulary. Frequent grammar errors.
Hedging absent or formulaic. IMRAD structure attempted but incomplete.

**Oral:** Can communicate in simple terms on academic topics.
Pronunciation generally intelligible. Frequent pauses for planning.

**Typical profile:** L1/L2 undergraduates, first year of specialist English.

---

## B2 — Independent User
**Written:** Can write clear, detailed texts on a range of subjects.
Adequate academic vocabulary (AWL sublist 1–4). Grammar mostly accurate.
Basic hedging present. IMRAD structure coherent if sometimes thin.

**Oral:** Can communicate with reasonable fluency and spontaneity.
Can participate in seminars and Q&A. Some errors under pressure.

**Typical profile:** L2/L3 undergraduates, Master 1 entry level.

---

## B2+ / C1 Transition
**Written:** Reliable IMRAD structure. Hedging appropriate.
Register consistent. AWL sublists 1–7. Noun phrases well-formed.
Citation integration functional.

**Typical profile:** Target level for Master 1–2 scientific English courses.

---

## C1 — Proficient User
**Written:** Can express ideas fluently and spontaneously.
Wide range of academic vocabulary used precisely. Complex noun phrases.
Nuanced hedging calibrated to evidence strength.
IMRAD moves executed with rhetorical sophistication.

**Oral:** Fluent, spontaneous, well-structured. Can handle Q&A confidently.

**Typical profile:** Doctoral students, researchers writing for publication.

---

## C2 — Mastery
**Written:** Can write with clarity, precision, and nuance.
Near-native range and accuracy. Genre conventions mastered.
Can adapt register to audience (specialist / non-specialist).

**Typical profile:** Native-level scientific writers; not a common course target.

---
